BIN306 Drug Design Lab: Viva Questions

6 questions for each exercise, Ex 1 to Ex 6. Each question is marked CONCEPT or PROCEDURE. The yellow box is the short answer to say in the viva.

How to use this

  1. Read the question. Cover the answer.
  2. Say your answer out loud.
  3. Check it against the yellow box.
  4. If you have only 10 minutes, read only the 10 facts below and the yellow boxes.

The 10 most important facts

  1. 2D = connectivity only. 3D = x, y, z coordinates, shape. Docking needs 3D.
  2. Energy minimization uses a force field to remove strain and clashes. It reaches a local minimum.
  3. Redocking = remove the crystal ligand, dock it back, compare. It validates the protocol.
  4. RMSD ≤ 2 Å = redocking successful.
  5. 1HSG = HIV protease + indinavir, X-ray, 1.9 Å. Homodimer (chains A and B). Active site is between the chains. Catalytic Asp25 / Asp25′.
  6. PDBQT = PDB + Q (partial charge) + T (AutoDock atom type) + torsion tree for the ligand.
  7. Grid: centred on the crystal ligand, spacing 0.375 Å. AutoGrid4 makes the maps (GPF → GLG). AutoDock4 docks (DPF → DLG).
  8. LGA = genetic algorithm (global search) + local search. 100 runs. Poses within 2 Å are one cluster.
  9. Flexible docking: ARG8 of chains A and B can move. Two receptor files: rigid and flex.
  10. MD order: pdb2gmx → editconf → solvate → genion → EM → NVT → NPT → production → analysis (RMSD, Rg, RMSF, H-bonds).

Ex 1: Drawing 2D and 3D Structures

Software: ChemSketch (ACD/Labs), MarvinSketch (ChemAxon), Avogadro

CONCEPT Q1. What is the difference between a 2D and a 3D structure?

  • 2D shows only which atoms are connected (connectivity). It is flat. No real bond lengths, angles or shape.
  • 3D has real x, y, z coordinates, so it has bond lengths, bond angles, shape and conformations.
  • Docking needs 3D because the molecule must physically fit into the protein pocket.
SAY THIS2D gives connectivity only. 3D gives coordinates, bond lengths, angles and shape. Docking needs 3D so the molecule can fit into the pocket.

CONCEPT Q2. Why is energy minimization done after converting 2D to 3D?

  • After conversion, the structure is strained: wrong bond lengths and angles, and some atoms clash.
  • A force field (for example MMFF94 or UFF in Avogadro) calculates the energy.
  • Atoms are moved step by step to lower the energy (steepest descent or conjugate gradient).
  • The result is a stable, realistic geometry that can be used for docking.
SAY THISTo remove strain and clashes. A force field lowers the energy step by step until a stable, realistic geometry is reached.

CONCEPT Q3. What is the difference between a local minimum and a global minimum?

  • Local minimum = the lowest energy in the nearby region (the nearest valley).
  • Global minimum = the lowest energy of all possible conformations (the deepest valley).
  • Minimization only goes downhill. It cannot cross energy barriers, so it stops at a local minimum.
  • Finding the global minimum needs a conformational search.
SAY THISLocal = nearest valley. Global = lowest of all. Minimization reaches only a local minimum because it cannot cross energy barriers.

CONCEPT Q4. What are .sk2, .mol and .pdb files used for?

  • .sk2: ChemSketch's own format. Only ChemSketch opens it.
  • .mol: MDL Molfile. Stores atoms, coordinates and bonds (connection table). Used to move the structure from ChemSketch to Avogadro.
  • .pdb (or .cml): 3D coordinate file. Used to save the optimised 3D structure.
SAY THIS.sk2 is for ChemSketch. .mol carries atoms and bonds into Avogadro. .pdb or .cml saves the final 3D structure.

PROCEDURE Q5. How do you draw paracetamol in ChemSketch and save it for Avogadro?

  1. Open ChemSketch in Structure mode.
  2. Draw a benzene ring with the ring tool.
  3. Add O on one carbon (gives OH).
  4. On the opposite carbon (para), add N, then C, then a double-bonded O, then CH₃ (gives NH–C(=O)–CH₃).
  5. Clean the structure. Check the formula: C₈H₉NO₂.
  6. File → Save As → .sk2. Then File → Export → MDL Molfile (.mol).
SAY THISBenzene ring, OH on one carbon, NHCOCH₃ on the para carbon, clean structure, save as .sk2, export as .mol.

PROCEDURE Q6. What are the steps in Avogadro?

  1. Open Avogadro. File → Open the .mol file.
  2. Add hydrogens if missing (Build → Add Hydrogens).
  3. Choose a force field (Extensions → Molecular Mechanics → Setup Force Field, for example MMFF94).
  4. Optimise the geometry (Extensions → Optimize Geometry). Watch the energy fall.
  5. Rotate and zoom to check the 3D shape.
  6. Save as .pdb or .cml.
SAY THISOpen the .mol, add hydrogens, set up the force field, optimise the geometry, check it in 3D, save as .pdb or .cml.

Extra: Aspirin C₉H₈O₄ (acetyloxy group −OCOCH₃ + COOH). Paracetamol C₈H₉NO₂. Ibuprofen C₁₃H₁₈O₂ (isobutyl + −CH(CH₃)COOH). Caffeine = 1,3,7-trimethylxanthine. Statins inhibit HMG-CoA reductase. Amoxicillin = β-lactam fused to thiazolidine.

Ex 2: Rigid Docking (1HSG + Indinavir, AutoDock4)

Receptor is fixed. Ligand can move, rotate and twist its torsions.

CONCEPT Q1. What is redocking and why is it done first?

  • Remove the co-crystallized ligand and dock it back into the same binding site.
  • Compare the docked pose with the crystal pose: RMSD, visual overlay, key interactions.
  • RMSD ≤ 2 Å means success.
  • It validates the protocol (preparation, grid, parameters) before docking unknown molecules.
SAY THISRedocking puts the crystal ligand back into its own site. If RMSD is ≤ 2 Å, the protocol is validated and can be trusted for new molecules.

PROCEDURE Q2. How do you prepare the receptor?

  1. Download 1HSG.pdb from RCSB PDB.
  2. Save the ligand separately (indinavir_crystal_pose.pdb). It is the RMSD reference.
  3. Remove the ligand, water and other heteroatoms.
  4. Keep both chains A and B (the active site is between them).
  5. In AutoDockTools: add polar hydrogens, add Kollman charges, merge non-polar hydrogens.
  6. Save as 1HSG_receptor.pdbqt.
SAY THISSave the ligand, remove ligand and water, keep both chains, add polar H and charges, merge non-polar H, save as PDBQT.

CONCEPT Q3. What is PDBQT and why are polar hydrogens and charges needed?

  • PDBQT = PDB + Q (partial charge) + T (AutoDock atom type).
  • For the ligand it also stores the torsion tree (ROOT, BRANCH, TORSDOF).
  • X-ray structures have no hydrogens. H-bonds need H atoms on N and O.
  • Charges are needed to calculate electrostatic energy.
SAY THISPDBQT adds charges, atom types and rotatable bonds. Polar H are needed for H-bonds and charges for electrostatics.

PROCEDURE Q4. How do you prepare the ligand?

  1. Use a working copy of the crystal ligand (do not change the original).
  2. Add hydrogens. Assign Gasteiger charges.
  3. Detect the root atom and the rotatable bonds.
  4. Make ring bonds and amide bonds non-rotatable.
  5. Record the number of active torsions. Save as indinavir.pdbqt.
SAY THISAdd H, Gasteiger charges, set root and torsions (rings and amides fixed), save as PDBQT. More torsions = bigger search space.

PROCEDURE Q5. How do you set up the grid and run the docking?

  1. Grid box centred on the crystal ligand. Spacing 0.375 Å. Big enough for the ligand plus space to move.
  2. Save the GPF. Run: autogrid4 -p 1HSG_indinavir.gpf -l 1HSG_indinavir.glg
  3. AutoGrid4 makes one map per ligand atom type, plus electrostatic (e) and desolvation (d) maps.
  4. Make the DPF: Lamarckian Genetic Algorithm, 100 runs.
  5. Run: autodock4 -p 1HSG_indinavir.dpf -l 1HSG_indinavir.dlg
SAY THISGrid on the crystal ligand, 0.375 Å. AutoGrid4 makes energy maps (GPF → GLG). AutoDock4 docks with LGA, 100 runs (DPF → DLG).

CONCEPT Q6. How do you analyse the result?

  • Binding energy: predicted by the scoring function (kcal/mol). More negative = more favourable. Not experimental.
  • Ki (estimated inhibition constant) = exp(ΔG/RT). Also predicted.
  • Cluster: poses within 2 Å RMSD are grouped. A big cluster = the same pose was found many times = reliable.
  • RMSD = √(Σd²/N) over heavy atoms, compared with the crystal pose. ≤ 2 Å = success.
  • Also check the overlay and whether key interactions are kept (central OH to Asp25/Asp25′).
SAY THISUse energy, cluster size, RMSD and interactions together. Energy alone is not enough because the scoring function is approximate.

Extra: 1HSG = X-ray, 1.9 Å, ligand ID MK1, chains A + B (99 residues each). HIV protease is an aspartyl protease. It cuts the Gag-Pol polyprotein, so blocking it gives immature, non-infectious virus. Flap residues Ile50/Ile50′. LGA = genetic algorithm + local search; "Lamarckian" because the improved pose is written back into the genes. Why 100 runs: the search is random, so many runs check convergence. Limitations: no receptor flexibility, simple water model, uncertain protonation, approximate scoring, poor entropy.

Ex 3: Flexible Docking (ARG8 A and B)

Same system as Ex 2, but the side chains of ARG8 in both chains can move.

CONCEPT Q1. What is the difference between rigid and flexible docking?

  • Rigid: the whole receptor is fixed. Only the ligand moves.
  • Flexible: selected receptor side chains (here ARG8 A and B) can also rotate.
  • Flexible is more realistic, but the search space is bigger and it is slower.
SAY THISRigid keeps the receptor fixed. Flexible lets chosen side chains move: more realistic, but a bigger search.

PROCEDURE Q2. How do you prepare the flexible residues?

  1. Clean the receptor as in Ex 2 (remove ligand and water, keep both chains, polar H, charges).
  2. In AutoDockTools: Flexible Residues → choose the receptor → select ARG8 in chains A and B.
  3. Choose which side-chain bonds can rotate.
  4. Save two files: 1HSG_rigid.pdbqt and 1HSG_flex_ARG8AB.pdbqt.
SAY THISSelect ARG8 of chains A and B as flexible, then save one rigid file and one flex file.

CONCEPT Q3. Why are two receptor files needed?

  • AutoGrid makes maps only for the part that does not move, so it uses only the rigid file.
  • The flexible side chains move during docking, so they cannot be in fixed maps.
  • The flex file is given later, in the DPF.
SAY THISMaps are pre-calculated, so only the fixed part goes to AutoGrid. The moving side chains go into the DPF.

CONCEPT Q4. Why are only a few residues made flexible?

  • Every flexible residue adds torsions.
  • More torsions = much bigger search space = slower and less reliable search.
  • So only key binding-site residues are chosen.
SAY THISEach flexible residue adds torsions, and the search space grows very fast, so only key residues are chosen.

PROCEDURE Q5. How do you set up and run flexible docking?

  1. Load 1HSG_rigid.pdbqt. Centre the grid on the crystal ligand. Spacing 0.375 Å.
  2. Make the box big enough for the ligand and the movement of ARG8 A and B.
  3. Run: autogrid4 -p 1HSG_indinavir_flex.gpf -l 1HSG_indinavir_flex.glg
  4. DPF: rigid receptor + flex file + ligand, LGA, 100 runs.
  5. Run: autodock4 -p 1HSG_indinavir_flex.dpf -l 1HSG_indinavir_flex.dlg
SAY THISGrid on the rigid file, big enough for ARG8 to move. The DPF includes rigid + flex + ligand. LGA, 100 runs.

CONCEPT Q6. Is flexible docking better if its score is more negative?

  • Not automatically.
  • Also compare RMSD, cluster size, kept interactions, and whether the ARG8 side chains look realistic.
  • Molecular dynamics can add full protein flexibility and time.
SAY THISNo. A better score alone does not prove it. Check RMSD, clustering, interactions and side-chain shape.

Ex 4: Protein–Ligand Interaction Analysis

Python (Colab) → RDKit → PyMOL (3D) → ProteinsPlus PoseView (2D)

CONCEPT Q1. What is in a DLG file and how do you choose a pose?

  • The DLG has every docked pose: run number, binding energy, coordinates and the clustering histogram.
  • Each pose starts at DOCKED: MODEL and ends at DOCKED: ENDMDL.
  • Energy line: Estimated Free Energy of Binding = … kcal/mol.
  • Choose using both the lowest energy and the cluster size.
SAY THISThe DLG holds all poses, energies and clusters. Choose the pose using energy and cluster size together.

PROCEDURE Q2. What does the Python program do?

  1. Read the DLG file line by line.
  2. Find each model between MODEL and ENDMDL. Store its run number, energy and coordinates.
  3. Remove incomplete models. Pick the model with the most negative energy (automatically, not by hand).
  4. Remove the DOCKED: prefix. Write 1HSG_best_pose.pdbqt and a PDB copy.
  5. Rename the ligand residue to LIG.
  6. Combine with 1HSG_receptor.pdb → 1HSG_docked_complex.pdb. Print a summary.
SAY THISParse every model, store run, energy and coordinates, pick the lowest energy, strip "DOCKED:", rename to LIG, merge with the receptor.

CONCEPT Q3. Why use PDB (not PDBQT) for RDKit, and why make an SDF?

  • PDBQT has extra AutoDock columns (charge, atom type) that RDKit cannot read reliably.
  • PDB has no bond orders.
  • SDF stores bond orders, explicit hydrogens and formal charges, so PoseView reads the chemistry correctly.
SAY THISRDKit can't read PDBQT reliably. SDF keeps bond orders and hydrogens, which PDB does not.

PROCEDURE Q4. What are the RDKit steps in Colab?

  1. Install RDKit (pip install rdkit) and import it.
  2. Read 1HSG_best_pose.pdb (keep the coordinates).
  3. Check it is one molecule. Fix bond orders (for example from a SMILES template).
  4. Add hydrogens with AddHs(addCoords=True), so heavy atoms stay in place.
  5. Do not run a full geometry optimisation (it would move the docked pose).
  6. Write 1HSG_best_pose_H.sdf and download it.
SAY THISRead the PDB, check one molecule, fix bond orders, add H without moving heavy atoms, write SDF.

PROCEDURE Q5. What do you do in PyMOL?

  1. Run the script: @visualize_1HSG_complex.pml
  2. It loads the complex, selects the ligand (resn LIG) and residues within 4 Å (binding site).
  3. Shows protein as cartoon, ligand and binding site as sticks.
  4. Shows H-bonds with distance … mode=2 (polar contacts) as dashed lines.
  5. Labels residues, then saves the session (.pse) and a 300 dpi PNG.
SAY THISLoad the complex, show the 4 Å binding site as sticks, show polar contacts with mode=2, label, save the image.

CONCEPT Q6. How are PyMOL and PoseView different?

  • PyMOL: 3D. Best for seeing where the ligand sits in space. Shows polar contacts.
  • PoseView: 2D diagram. Best for a summary of all interactions (H-bonds, hydrophobic, aromatic).
  • They may not match because they use different distance and angle rules and handle hydrogens differently.
  • In 1HSG, both chains contribute, and the ligand is deeply buried under the flaps.
SAY THISPyMOL shows 3D space. PoseView summarises interactions in 2D. They can differ because their rules for interactions are different.

Ex 5: MD Simulation Setup (GROMACS)

Order: pdb2gmx → editconf → solvate → genion → EM → NVT → NPT → production

CONCEPT Q1. What is MD and why do it after docking?

  • MD (molecular dynamics) moves every atom over time using Newton's laws and a force field.
  • Docking gives one still picture. MD shows whether the complex stays stable over time.
  • MD includes protein flexibility and water.
SAY THISDocking is one snapshot. MD checks stability over time, with flexibility and water.

PROCEDURE Q2. How is the topology made?

  1. gmx pdb2gmx -f 1LZM.pdb -o 1LZM_processed.gro -water spce
  2. Choose force field AMBER99SB-ILDN. Output: .gro (coordinates), topol.top (topology), posre.itp (restraints).
  3. pdb2gmx only knows protein residues, so the ligand topology is made separately (PRODRG, CGenFF or ACPYPE).
  4. Add #include "benzene.itp" to topol.top, and add the ligand under [ molecules ].
SAY THISpdb2gmx makes the protein topology with a force field and water model. The ligand topology is made separately and included in topol.top.

PROCEDURE Q3. How do you make the box, add water and add ions?

  1. Box: gmx editconf -c -d 1.0 -bt cubic. Protein centred, at least 1.0 nm from the edge, so it does not touch its own periodic image (PBC).
  2. Water: gmx solvate -cs spc216.gro -p topol.top
  3. Ions: gmx grompp -f ions.mdp to make ions.tpr, then gmx genion -pname NA -nname CL -neutral
  4. Ions make the total charge zero (neutral system).
SAY THISeditconf makes a cubic box with 1.0 nm space, solvate adds water, genion adds Na⁺/Cl⁻ to neutralise.

CONCEPT Q4. Why is energy minimization done before MD?

  • After adding water and ions, some atoms are too close (clashes) and forces are huge.
  • Minimization (steepest descent) removes the clashes.
  • It stops when the maximum force is below 1000 kJ/mol/nm (emtol).
  • Without it, the simulation can crash.
SAY THISTo remove clashes and very high forces. Steepest descent until max force < 1000 kJ/mol/nm.

CONCEPT Q5. What is the difference between NVT and NPT?

  • NVT: constant number of atoms, volume, temperature. Brings temperature to 300 K (V-rescale thermostat). 100 ps.
  • NPT: constant number of atoms, pressure, temperature. Brings pressure to 1 bar (Parrinello-Rahman barostat) and fixes density. 100 ps.
  • Both use position restraints (-DPOSRES) so the protein stays still while water settles.
SAY THISNVT fixes temperature (300 K). NPT then fixes pressure (1 bar) and density. Protein is restrained during both.

PROCEDURE Q6. How is the production run done?

  1. gmx grompp -f md.mdp -c npt.gro -p topol.top -o md_0_1.tpr
  2. gmx mdrun -deffnm md_0_1
  3. 500,000 steps × 2 fs = 1 ns. No position restraints.
  4. LINCS constrains bonds, which allows a 2 fs time step.
SAY THISgrompp makes the .tpr from md.mdp and npt.gro, mdrun runs it: 1 ns at 2 fs per step.

Files: .mdp = parameters, .gro = coordinates, .top/.itp = topology, .tpr = binary run input, .xtc = trajectory, .edr = energies, .log = log, .xvg = graph data. Units: GROMACS uses nm, ps, kJ/mol. AutoDock uses Å and kcal/mol.

Ex 6: MD Analysis

All results are .xvg files, plotted with xmgrace (Grace).

CONCEPT Q1. What does RMSD tell you in MD?

  • How much the protein backbone moves away from the starting structure over time.
  • It rises at first, then should reach a plateau (stays flat). A plateau means the system is stable.
  • Ligand RMSD shows whether the ligand stays in its pose.
SAY THISRMSD measures deviation from the start. A plateau means the complex is stable.

CONCEPT Q2. What is radius of gyration (Rg)?

  • How compact the protein is (spread of atoms around its centre).
  • Stable Rg = protein stays folded. Rising Rg = unfolding or opening.
SAY THISRg measures compactness. A stable Rg means the protein stays folded.

CONCEPT Q3. What is RMSF and how is it different from RMSD?

  • RMSF = how much each residue moves around its average position.
  • RMSD is over time (one value per frame). RMSF is per residue (one value per residue).
  • Loops and the ends (termini) have high RMSF. Binding-site residues with low RMSF are held by the ligand.
SAY THISRMSD is per time, RMSF is per residue. RMSF shows which parts are flexible.

CONCEPT Q4. What does the hydrogen-bond analysis show?

  • The number of protein–ligand H-bonds in each frame.
  • H-bonds that stay through the whole run are important for binding.
  • Distance analysis (ligand to residue) shows whether the ligand stays in the pocket.
SAY THISIt counts H-bonds over time. Bonds that persist show stable binding.

PROCEDURE Q5. What commands are used for the analysis?

  1. RMSD: gmx rms -s md_0_1.tpr -f md_0_1.xtc -o rmsd.xvg -tu ns
  2. Rg: gmx gyrate -s md_0_1.tpr -f md_0_1.xtc -o gyrate.xvg
  3. RMSF: gmx rmsf -s md_0_1.tpr -f md_0_1.xtc -o rmsf.xvg -res
  4. H-bonds: gmx hbond -s md_0_1.tpr -f md_0_1.xtc -num hbonds.xvg
  5. Distance: gmx distance … -oall dist.xvg
  6. Plot each one: xmgrace rmsd.xvg
SAY THISgmx rms, gyrate, rmsf, hbond and distance, each with the .tpr and .xtc, giving .xvg files to plot with xmgrace.

CONCEPT Q6. Advantages and limitations of GROMACS?

  • Advantages: free and open source, very fast, uses GPUs, many force fields, many built-in analysis tools.
  • Limitations: command line only, ligand topology needs other tools, short time scales (ns), accuracy depends on the force field.
SAY THISFast, free and has many analysis tools. But it is command-line, needs separate ligand topology, and only reaches short time scales.
